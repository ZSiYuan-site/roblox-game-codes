# How to Use the Pips Answer Archive Without Spoiling a Board

Pips is a daily domino puzzle from The New York Times Games. You get a board of coloured regions and a set of dominoes, and the job is to place every domino so that each region follows its own rule. Because a new puzzle appears every day at midnight local time, it is easy to fall behind. The archive exists for exactly that situation, but it can also spoil a board you have not finished, so there is a knack to using it.

## What the rules ask of you

The [how to play guide](https://puzzles.newgamescout.com/how-to-play/) sets out the five region types. A number means the pips in that region add up to the number. An equals sign means every half domino shows the same number. A not-equals sign means every half is different. A less-than or greater-than sign means the total is under or over the number. A region with no colour has no rule at all. Seven of those words do most of the work on any given board.

## A worked example from the archive

Take the board for August 18, 2025. The [Pips answer for August 18, 2025](https://puzzles.newgamescout.com/answers/2025-08-18/) has three separate puzzles: Easy with four dominoes, Medium with five, and Hard with ten. The hints come before the answers, which is the right order to read them. On the Easy board the cell at row one, column four is a five, and the 6-6 domino sits with the sixes in row three. On the Medium board the single cell at row three, column two is a two. Those one-cell regions are the fastest way into any board.

## When to open the archive

The [Pips answer archive](https://puzzles.newgamescout.com/archive/) holds 459 puzzles, newest first, and each day includes hints plus the solved Easy, Medium and Hard boards. If you only want to check one stubborn region, read the hint rather than the full solution. If you want to practise on a style of board you keep missing, pick an older date and work it through from scratch. If you simply want [pips answers today](https://puzzles.newgamescout.com/) after you have finished, the front page is the shortest route.

## A habit that works

Finish the one-cell regions first, then place the biggest dominoes such as 6-6 where a high total is required. Doubles usually belong in an equals region with several cells. Corners and dead ends have only one way a domino can lie, so they are good places to start when the middle is stuck. Only then compare your work with a hint. This keeps the archive as a tool rather than a crutch.

## Why dates matter

The archive switches to a new day at midnight US Eastern time, while the game itself rolls over at midnight in your local time. If you live outside that zone, the date on an answer page can be one step ahead of or behind the puzzle you see. Checking yesterday's page is often the fix. The site is an unofficial fan site and is not affiliated with The New York Times, but the boards come from the same puzzle data the game uses, so the solutions match.
