# Thanksgiving Trivia: Who Was at the 1621 Feast and How It Became a Holiday

Thanksgiving trivia is easy to write badly, because everyone knows turkey and pie. The [Thanksgiving trivia](https://trivia.resuhive.com/general-knowledge/thanksgiving/) set is better when it asks about history. Here are the questions that teach something, with the explanation each one gives.

## The food questions

Turkey is the common main dish, which is why the day is sometimes called Turkey Day. The tart red berry made into a sauce is the cranberry, which grows in bogs and has long been a staple on Thanksgiving tables. The most traditional dessert is pumpkin pie, and the page notes that pumpkin is a native American crop and a classic pie filling.

## The 1621 feast

The Native American people who shared the 1621 harvest feast with the Plymouth colonists were the Wampanoag. The page adds that about 90 Wampanoag men joined the feast, according to the colonist Edward Winslow. Another question asks which Patuxet man taught the Pilgrims how to plant corn and fish. That was Squanto, also known as Tisquantum, who spoke English and helped the colonists survive.

## How it became national

Two questions explain the holiday's path to the calendar.

- Abraham Lincoln proclaimed a national day of Thanksgiving in 1863, during the Civil War. His proclamation set the last Thursday of November as a national day of thanks.
- Sarah Josepha Hale, the editor of Godey's Lady's Book, campaigned for years for a national Thanksgiving holiday, writing letters to officials.

Those two answers make a nice pair. One explains who pushed for it, the other who made it official.
