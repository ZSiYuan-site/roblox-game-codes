# Pips Answers Archive: Catch Up on Every Daily NYT Pips Puzzle


Streaks matter in daily puzzles, and a missed day can feel like a broken record. If you skipped a Pips puzzle or want to practice on old boards, the [Pips Answers archive](https://puzzles.newgamescout.com/archive/) is a handy place to look. This post explains what it contains and how to use it well.

## What is in the archive

The archive lists every Pips puzzle the site has covered. At the time of writing that is 457 puzzles, newest first, grouped by month starting with November 2026 and moving back through October 2026 and earlier. Each day opens to hints and the solved Easy, Medium and Hard boards. The home page, the [pips answer today](https://puzzles.newgamescout.com/) page, always shows the current date.

## Using it as practice

Old boards are good training. Pick a date from a few weeks ago, try the Hard board without help, and open hints only when you are truly stuck. Because every day has three boards, you get one quick Easy board for warming up and a long Hard one for a challenge. Dominoes, regions and rules are the same every day, so skills carry over.

## The three hints

Each board gives three hints before the answer. The first describes the board: the number of dominoes and the count of sum, equals, greater-than and less-than regions. The second names a region to start with and tells you what goes there. The third pairs a specific domino with its cells. Because the hints are numbered, you can stop after one and keep thinking.

## Reading solved boards

The answer for each board shows the filled grid with dashed lines that join the two halves of each domino. Underneath is a plain list such as the 3-3 domino with a 3 at row 1, column 2 and a 3 at row 2, column 2. Use the list to verify your own placement. If your solution disagrees, find the first domino that differs, and that usually reveals the mistake.

## Rules refresher

New to the game? The [how to play Pips](https://puzzles.newgamescout.com/how-to-play/) page explains each region symbol and gives three tips: fill one-cell regions first, look for the biggest dominoes like 6-6, and expect doubles in large equals regions. There is also a [yesterday page](https://puzzles.newgamescout.com/yesterday/) for last day's answers.

One last note: this is an unofficial fan site and is not affiliated with The New York Times. Pips belongs to NYT Games. Search for the daily puzzle there, and treat the archive as your study notes. If you search for pips answers, you want a page that respects your puzzle time, which is why the hint-first layout is a good design.
