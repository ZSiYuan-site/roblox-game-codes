# How to Tell If a Class Action Settlement Is Real, and Where to Find Open Ones


A settlement email can be genuine, or it can be a scam wearing a logo. Before you click, you want two things: a way to verify that the settlement exists, and a place to see its deadline and rules. Settlement Lookup is built for the second, and its guides help with the first. Start with the main list of [class action settlements open now](https://settlements.resuhive.com/) and use the checks below.

## Check 1: does the settlement appear on an independent list

The site tracks 590 settlements and says that 454 were checked against the official settlement website. If the settlement named in your email is not there, that is not proof that it is fake, but it is a reason to slow down. Search by company name. A real case will usually have a named company, a clear class definition and a claim deadline.

## Check 2: does the deadline and amount match

On each card the site shows the claim-by date and the amount. A real notice says what you can receive, for example up to $251 for unwanted texts, or up to $2,500 for a data breach. Be careful with amounts that sound too good. If an email promises a large guaranteed payment and asks for a bank login, that is not how settlements work.

## Check 3: never pay to claim

Genuine class action claims are free. The tracker is not a law firm and it never collects your information, which is a good model to look for in any site you use. File only through the official settlement site named in the court-approved notice.

## Check 4: understand the timeline

Many people worry when a claim is filed and nothing arrives. Settlements pay after a judge grants final approval, and that can take months. The [payout dates](https://settlements.resuhive.com/payout-dates/) page lists settlements whose claim period has ended and those with a final approval hearing coming up. On November 17, 2026 it showed two settlements paying with a date posted and 386 whose claim period had closed without a published payment date.

## Check 5: use the right shortcuts

If you just want the quick wins, use [settlement claim deadlines](https://settlements.resuhive.com/closing-soon/) to see what closes this week, and the no-proof list to find claims that need no receipts. The site also has guides called Is That Settlement Email Legit, How Class Action Settlements Work, and Settlement Payout Timeline, which cover the same questions in more detail.

## The short version

Verify first, file on the official site, never pay, keep your confirmation, and be patient about payment. A few minutes with a tracker can protect you from scams and make sure you do not miss real money. This page is a general summary and not legal advice, and the details of each case are on the official notice.
